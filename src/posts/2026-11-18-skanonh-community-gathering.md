# A Community Gathering for Indigenous Families at Skä•noñh

Indigenous families in the Syracuse City School District, you are invited. Join us on **Sunday, December 6, at 11:00 AM** for a community gathering at the Skä•noñh Great Law of Peace Center in Liverpool. Come to learn, connect, and get involved. Bring your children, your elders, and your questions.

Families will come for different reasons. You may want to know what cultural programs exist for your kids. You may want to meet other families who share your ties to this land. You may want a warm place to spend a winter Sunday morning. Any of those is reason enough to come.

## Event Details

- **What:** Community Gathering for Indigenous families in the Syracuse City School District
- **When:** Sunday, December 6, 2026, at 11:00 AM
- **Where:** Skä•noñh Great Law of Peace Center, 6680 Onondaga Lake Parkway, Liverpool, NY 13088
- **RSVP:** By **Monday, November 30**, to Tina at **315-901-2611**

Please RSVP so we can plan food and activities for everyone in your group.

## What to Expect

The morning has four parts:

1. **Learn about cultural history and programming.** Hear about the history held at Skä•noñh and the programs available to families.
2. **Explore opportunities for K–12 students.** Find out how students from kindergarten through high school can take part in cultural learning, events, and leadership.
3. **Tour the Skä•noñh Center.** Walk through the exhibits together as a family.
4. **Enjoy Native foods and activities for all ages.** Share a meal while the kids try hands-on activities.

## Why Skä•noñh

Skä•noñh (said "SCAN-oh") is an Onondaga greeting that means peace and wellness, [according to Syracuse.com](https://www.syracuse.com/north/2015/11/sneak_peek_ska-nonh_great_law.html). The center's first exhibit explains that the word also describes [a state of being](https://www.cnyhistory.org/programs/ska%E2%80%A2nonh-welcome/).

The center tells the story of the Haudenosaunee from the perspective of the Onondaga Nation. The Onondaga are the [keepers of the Central Fire](https://www.cnyhistory.org/visit/skanonh/), the spiritual and political center of the Haudenosaunee Confederacy. Exhibits cover Creation, the Great Law of Peace, and European contact. The Onondaga Historical Association runs the museum, which was [developed with and is staffed by Onondaga Nation members](https://www.syracuse.com/living/2025/05/traveling-exhibition-explores-american-democracy-and-haudenosaunee-influence-on-view-in-syracuse.html).

The site has a history too. For a generation it was Sainte Marie Among the Iroquois, a museum that told local history from the point of view of French Jesuit missionaries. In 2015 it reopened as Skä•noñh to share that history [from the Haudenosaunee perspective](https://www.onondaganation.org/news/2015/2991/). The stories once told here about Native people are now told by Native people. That makes it a fitting place for Indigenous families to gather.

## What Students Will See

The center's [Contact exhibit](https://www.cnyhistory.org/programs/contact/) covers treaties like the Two Row Wampum and Canandaigua. It also covers the Doctrine of Discovery, the Sullivan-Clinton Campaign, and boarding schools.

Many students may not meet these subjects in a textbook. On the tour, with family nearby, young people can ask questions and hear honest answers. Parents and grandparents can use what they see to start conversations at home.

## Look Closely at the Flyer

The flyer has three details worth noticing.

- **The Hiawatha Belt** sits at the top. It stands for the nations joined together under the Great Law of Peace.
- **Corn and beans** fill the lower corner. They stand for Haudenosaunee food and farming knowledge.
- **Asters and cedar** frame the invitation. They are plants of this region.
