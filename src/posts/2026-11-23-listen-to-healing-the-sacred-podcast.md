Look closely at the cover of our new podcast.

An eel curls through green water. Fish drift past in the light. Then you see the plastic bag riding on one fish's back, and the tire, the can, and the bottles on the lakebed.

Victoria Dickerson's artwork shows Onondaga Lake as we know it: full of life, and still holding what industry left in it. That is where _Healing the Sacred: Restoring Onondaga Lake through Story, Ceremony, and Public Scholarship_ begins. All 11 episodes are ready for you now.

## Why we made it

Onondaga Lake is the sacred site where _Gayanashagowa_, the Great Law of Peace, was established. Industry polluted it. The Onondaga Nation is asking for it back.

We believe the story of the lake belongs first to the people who carry it. So Betty Lyons Hill, Patricia Nolan Vogt, and Adam DJ Brett spent 11 episodes asking questions and listening to the answers.

## For teachers and study groups

Every episode comes with a transcript, discussion questions, and further reading. Teachers, there is a curriculum guide for you too. You will find all of it at [podcast.lakeback.org](https://podcast.lakeback.org/).

## Where we stand, and what you can do

We hold that returning Onondaga Lake to the Onondaga Nation is a vital step toward restoring this sacred place and toward healing for its people, the surrounding communities, and the natural world.

When you finish listening, visit [lakeback.org](https://lakeback.org/). That is the home of our campaign to return Maple Bay, on the northwest shore of the lake, to the Onondaga Nation. Businesses and community organizations can sign a letter of support there.
